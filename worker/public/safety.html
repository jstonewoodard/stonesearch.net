User Safety Guide

Effective: May 22, 2026 · Last updated: May 22, 2026

Stone Search is a meta-search engine. We aggregate results from third-party providers so you can find what you need quickly. We do not own the websites that appear in our results, and we cannot guarantee that any particular result is safe, accurate, or legal. This guide explains what we do, what we cannot do, and what you can do to protect yourself.

1. How we try to keep the Service safe

  • We follow the safe-search defaults of our upstream providers and pass safety filters through to your results.
  • We label sponsored results as “Ad” or “Sponsored” so you can tell paid placements from organic results.
  • We use rate-limiting and abuse detection to reduce scraping, denial-of-service attacks, and automated abuse.
  • We honor takedown notices that meet the requirements in our Terms of Service.
  • We review user reports of harmful or unlawful content and act on them in good faith.

2. What we cannot do

  • We do not control the websites that appear in our results.
  • We cannot guarantee every page is virus-free, factually correct, age-appropriate, or non-deceptive.
  • We cannot remove content from a third-party website — we can only de-list it from our results.
  • We are not a substitute for professional advice in medical, legal, financial, mental-health, or safety-critical situations.

3. Searching safely — practical tips

3.1 Spotting phishing and scams

  • Look at the domain before you click. “apple-support.help-desk.xyz” is not apple.com.
  • Be suspicious of urgent language, unexpected prizes, or threats in result snippets and on the destination page.
  • Type addresses of important sites directly into your browser — especially banking, government, and login pages.
  • Never enter passwords, credit card numbers, or government IDs into a site you reached from a search result unless you’re completely sure of the destination.

3.2 Downloads

  • Be cautious about downloading software, especially “free” tools from unfamiliar sites. Prefer the official vendor site.
  • Inspect file extensions. A “document” that ends in .exe, .scr, or .vbs is almost certainly not a document.
  • Keep your operating system, browser, and antivirus tools current.

3.3 Privacy on the open web

  • Many websites track you. Tracker-blocking browsers (Brave, Firefox with strict tracking protection, Safari with cross-site tracking prevention) reduce this.
  • Consider a VPN if you don’t want sites to see your IP.
  • Queries that include personal information (names, addresses, account numbers) leave a record in our logs and our upstream provider’s logs. Avoid putting personal info into queries when you don’t have to.

3.4 Children online

  • If a child uses your device, enable family-safety tools at the OS level (Screen Time on Apple, Family Link on Android/Google, Family Safety on Microsoft).
  • Stone Search does not currently provide a separate child mode.

5. Special categories of report

5.1 Child sexual abuse material (CSAM)

If you encounter CSAM in our results or anywhere else online, report it directly to the U.S. National Center for Missing and Exploited Children CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org, or to your country’s equivalent service. You may also email [CONTACT EMAIL] so we can de-list the URL from our results immediately. Do not download, save, or forward the material — doing so is itself illegal.

5.2 Copyright infringement (DMCA)

Send notices to our designated DMCA agent as described in §11 of our Terms of Service.

5.3 Privacy and personal-information takedowns

If a result links to a page that exposes your personal information (doxxing, non-consensual intimate imagery, leaked credentials), email [CONTACT EMAIL] with the URL and a brief description. We may de-list the URL while you work to have the content removed from the source site.

5.4 Defamation

We are not in a position to adjudicate factual disputes. Your remedy is generally with the third-party publisher and, where applicable, the courts. We may de-list pages subject to a valid court order.

6. If you are in danger

Stone Search is a search engine, not a crisis service. If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency number — 911 in the U.S., 112 in the EU and UK, or your local equivalent. Crisis support is available through services such as the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in the U.S. (call or text 988), and equivalent services in many other countries. We can help you find such resources through search, but we cannot provide crisis support ourselves.

7. Account safety (when accounts launch)

The Service today does not require an account to search. If we add accounts in the future:

  • Use a strong, unique password and enable two-factor authentication when offered.
  • Treat unexpected password-reset emails as potential phishing — go to the site directly instead of clicking the email link.
  • Stone Search will never ask for your password by email or chat.
